Topology: making geometry, not just moving it

These tools add, remove and join patches. They are in the Modeling, Topology (Edit Mode) and Tools boxes. The examples use cheetah.sgm.

The rule: two patches are joined only when they share all four control points of a side. A side used by one patch is open, and the Object Editor will not load it. Corners in the same place must still be welded.

Patches are selectable

  1. Press Tab, then 3.
  2. Click a patch. Box, lasso, circle, Select Linked and Grow / Shrink work too. Alt+Z selects through the body.
  3. H hides patches, Shift+H hides the unselected, Alt+H unhides.

Delete Patch and Fill Hole

  1. Select the patches.
  2. Use Modeling > Delete Patch or X > Patches. A patch on a connection ring is refused.
  3. Close the hole, or Validate blocks it.
  4. Use Topology > Fill Hole. With several holes, select a corner of one first. A hole needs an even number of sides, at least four.

Add Quad and Weld

Add Quad grows a patch on each selected open side. To close a gap, move the new corners into place and Weld.

  1. Select an open side and press Topology > Add Quad.
  2. Move the two free corners onto the far corners of the hole.
  3. Select all four and press Weld. Threshold 0 means 1 % of the creature's size. Weld Target merges into the active corner instead.
  4. Welding two open sides also merges their handles, which closes the seam.

Weld refuses to collapse a patch, give a side three patches, or merge two corners of one ring.

Subdivide: more patches where you need them

Subdivide splits each selected patch into four without changing the shape. The split carries on through neighbouring patches so no gaps form.

It refuses if the split would cross a connection ring. On the cheetah, no torso patch can be subdivided.

Extrude Region

Extrude Region lifts the selected patches and builds walls around them. In the redo panel set Amount (height) and Outline (scale of the top), or press G to move the lifted points. To drag at once, search for Extrude Region (Drag) (needs Developer Extras).

Mirror to Whole

Mirror to Whole builds the other half from one half. It mirrors the selected patches (or all) across X = 0, joins the centre seam, and uses the matching left or right bones and limbs. Snap Seam to Plane (on by default) puts seam points on X = 0.

  1. Delete the connections whose rings reach into the unwanted half, then delete that half's patches.
  2. Deselect everything and press Topology > Mirror to Whole.

It is refused if the half crosses X = 0 or the other side exists. For a finished creature use Symmetrize (X).

Attach: two creatures into one

In Object Mode, select the other creature, make yours active and press Tools > Attach. Choose the Limb and Bone for the new parts.

  • The other object and its rig are removed.
  • Its connection rings are dropped.
  • Materials are matched by name.
  • Seams are not joined. Weld them yourself.

What it costs: the display-vertex ceiling

The game limits a patch mesh to 12,288 display vertices. A patch costs about 16 where its seams weld and 25 where it has its own texture island.

  • Combining adds donor limbs to the same mesh. To stay safe, use at most 6,584 for the torso and 7,879 for the limbs.
  • Validate reports your real count; patch count alone does not tell you.
  • Attach refuses to exceed 767 patches.
  • No bevel or chamfer; Outline is the only taper.
  • No bridge, and no way to cap a loop except Fill Hole.
  • No primitives. Start from a stock creature, New Creature (From Scratch), Convert Mesh to Creature or Fit Patches to Mesh.
  • Subdivide cannot cut a ring side. Deleting the connection and re-picking it changes the socket.
  • Smoothing groups and material IDs cannot be edited.
