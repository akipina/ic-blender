# gmax parity: what blender_native has, what it lacks, and how the workflow should feel

*Assessed 2026-09-13; re-scored 2026-09-14 (New Creature, engine normals, clips as Actions, the stock rulebook, the combiner wiring), 2026-09-15 (Boner B1-B7 and C8) and 2026-09-16 (A1-A5, D9, D10, E1) -- every change is logged at the end of the verdict. Scored against the community guide, Relic's Boner documentation, the Autodesk Editable Patch reference and the measured stock data (sources in section 7). Statuses: **have** = present and comparable; **partial** = present but weaker or different in a way the artist feels; **missing**; **Blender** = Blender supplies it natively; **n/a** = not needed in this design.*

## Verdict

Across the 102 gmax/Boner features that the legacy process actually uses: **40 have**, **32 partial**, **10 missing**, 11 covered by Blender itself, 9 not applicable (counted from the tables in section 2, so the number can always be re-derived; the re-score log below tracks every change, and the original count was 20 / 25 / 36). Of the 18 steps of the guide's creature-from-scratch process, **7 work as well as or better than gmax and 11 work partially** -- none is impossible in the add-on any more.

The add-on is identical to gmax in what it stores and in the surface that data defines (and, since the engine normals landed, in how that surface is shaded), and it beats gmax on the axes gmax never had: lossless re-import of shipped creatures, and a combine preview plus offline combine checks that run the game's combiner rules on the creature while it is being edited. The hand and the making are no longer where it falls down: B5 and B6 gave the surface gmax's response to a drag (a corner carries its handles, interiors are automatic, a patch can be clicked, outlined, hidden and deleted), B7 gave the topology tools (delete, weld, attach, mirror-to-whole, subdivide, add quad, fill hole, extrude region), C8 gave the bone edits, and A1 gave the start: **block out any shape in Blender and Fit Patches to Mesh turns it into a riggable patch creature at a budget you choose**, so building a creature from a box is possible now -- by a different road than the guide's, but the same destination. Reshaping a stock creature is excellent, **New Creature** starts a combinable one from a scaffold, and **Retarget** carries a whole creature -- rig, limbs, rings and all -- onto a scanned mesh.

**What is still simply absent** (the short list, and the same one §4's Surface-properties row asks for): **material IDs per patch** and **smoothing groups per patch** -- the two per-patch attributes gmax edits and we only preserve -- plus **bevel**, **Add Tri**, **Detach** and **Reset Handles**. Nothing on that list is architectural; they are operators nobody has written yet.

## 1. The legacy process, step by step

This is the sequence the community guide teaches (chapters 3 to 12) with the Relic rules folded in. It is the yardstick for 'easy to use': the add-on should let an artist follow these steps in this order, with these words.

It loads in the Object Editor and combines both ways in ComboTest.exe and in the combine tiers. Still missing: a wolf / lion template choice, an empty-pool or box start. ## 3. How the current process works, and why it does not feel like gmax

1. **Import, Tab, drag, export is the whole story.** The add-on is superb at the one thing gmax could never do: open a shipped .sgm losslessly, show the exact surface, and write back only what moved. Every stock creature round-trips byte for byte. That is the right foundation and nothing below argues for changing it.
2. **Starting a creature is a scaffold, not a box.** *(Answered by A1, 2026-09-16, though not the gmax way: block out any shape with Blender's own tools and **Fit Patches to Mesh** makes it a riggable patch creature at a budget -- a box's own quads become 6 patches. The paragraph stands as the record of the gap it describes: the box does not simply *become* an editable patch, it is fitted.)* The guide's first hour is: copy the wolf project, keep its bones, delete its mesh, draw a box at the origin, convert it to a patch. New Creature now gives a canonical skeleton, a connected body with stock-layout rings, the standard clips and IK chains, written to a file you pick, and it combines both ways. What is missing is the box-and-extrude start and a template choice (wolf, lion). Convert Mesh to Creature remains a conversion utility: it borrows a template's skeleton (default snail), name, limbs and material table.
3. **The first drag feels broken to a gmax hand.** *(Fixed by B5, 2026-09-15: a corner drags its handles, interiors are automatic and hidden; the paragraph stands as the record.)* In gmax a vertex is a vertex plus its handles: drag it and the surface translates. In our pool the handles and the four interior points of every touching patch stayed where they were, so the surface pinched and dented around the moved point. The interior rule was worse than a feel problem: 100 % of stock patches obey Max's Auto Interior, and every edit we made broke it, so our exports were distinguishable from anything Relic shipped.
4. **Patches are invisible as objects.** *(Fixed by B6, 2026-09-15: a corner quad per patch makes Face select mode the patch level -- click, outline, box select with occlusion, hide, delete; the paragraph stands as the record.)* gmax's Patch level is where extrusion, deletion, hiding, limb painting and material IDs happen, and a selected patch shows a dark-red outline. Here a patch was 'selected' when its four corners happened to be selected, nothing highlighted it, and box select silently took far-side points because a mesh without faces had nothing to occlude.
5. ~~**The surface lies about smoothness.**~~ Fixed: the overlay and the bake weld normals the engine's way (along shared edges, and within a smoothing group at corners), so the seams the game averages away no longer show.
6. **Topology work has to leave the tool.** *(Fixed by B7, 2026-09-15: Weld / Weld Target, Delete Patch, Add Quad, Fill Hole, Subdivide with propagation, Extrude Region with Amount + Outline, Mirror to Whole and Attach, each maintaining the patch table and each one Undo step; the guide's core loop runs here now, and the exports load in the Object Editor. What is still missing is bevel, Add Tri and Detach. The paragraph stands as the record.)* Delete patch, weld the spine, attach the mirrored half, add a quad on an open edge, subdivide a region, bevel: none exist. The single Extrude grows one patch from one fixed side with no drag and no number. So the guide's core loop (extrude the plan, delete the inside, mirror, weld, check open edges) cannot be done here at all; the practical route today is to import a stock creature whose topology is already right and reshape it.
7. **Boner is spread across dropdowns.** *(Fixed by B1-B4, B6, B7 and C8, 2026-09-14/15: the Boner sub-panel has Bones / Limbs / Connections tabs with real lists -- create, rename, delete, re-class, set root and host bones, Add Stub Socket, Rename / Add / Delete / Re-parent Bone, IK chains and joint limits -- all journaled, undoable and exported through ICParse, with the engine tier loading the results. The paragraph stands as the record.)* Boner is five explicit sub-modes with lists you can create, edit, delete and rename. We have dropdown-driven operators that can only re-point what the source file already had: no new limb, no flags, no new connection. The skinning loop is the exception since B5: Boner > Bones picks a bone by clicking, colours the overlay by bone and has Assign / Select / Remove / Select Unassigned / Auto Skin. Guided ring picking exists (Pick Ring: along patch sides, closed at the slot's size, stock winding and start). The rules the docs stress are now mostly known to Validate (6 and 4 vertex rings, their start, winding and placement through the combine checks, the animation set and the universal bones through the rulebook), but not the 15 limb names or the 18 material IDs.
8. **Texturing is half there.** *(Half-fixed by A4, 2026-09-16: **Transfer Texture** gives a creature a per-patch atlas UV layout and bakes a source mesh's image onto it, so a creature fitted from a textured scan CAN be textured from nothing -- with one image over the whole creature. Assigning a material ID to a patch, loading a bitmap into a numbered slot and the per-ID size and depth rules are still absent.)* Painting the existing textures and editing UVs on a cage object both work. Assigning a material ID to a patch, loading a new bitmap into a slot, and the per-ID size and depth rules are absent, so a new creature cannot be textured from nothing.

## 4. How it should work

Organise the add-on the way the guide's chapters and Boner's sub-modes are organised, so the panel reads like the legacy process. Each row is one panel or mode, in workflow order.

*Status 2026-09-16: most of this table's **contents** now exist -- what has not happened is the **reorganisation**. The tools live in the SGM panel, its Boner tabs, the Edit-Mode Modeling / Topology boxes and the Tools box, not in panels named the way the guide's chapters are. Still genuinely absent from any panel: **Surface properties** (material ID per patch, the 32 smoothing-group buttons, Flip patch) and most of **Texturing** (the 18-ID navigator, bitmap per slot, the size table, in-place UV editing).*

| Panel | Contents |
|---|---|
| **Project** | New Creature (name; template wolf or lion; limbs, connections, material slots renamed to the new name; a box at the origin or an empty pool), Import SGM, Convert Mesh (template chooser, renamed on the way in, no temp-file source path). |
| **Model** | One mode switch for Vertex / Edge / Patch / Element, driven by picking on the overlay surface (patch = click the surface, edge = click near a boundary), with a patch outline and occlusion-aware box select from the overlay's depth. Filter Vertices/Vectors as a sticky state, Lock Handles, handles shown only for selected vertices, Show Interior Edges, View Steps 0/1/2 labelled LOD-1/2/3. |
| **Feel** | A drag moves a vertex with its handles; interiors are recomputed from the boundary during the drag and on commit (Auto Interior, with an opt-in Manual switch); Coplanar / Corner per vertex; Reset Handles; normals welded by smoothing group in the overlay and the bake so the surface looks as the game will shade it. |
| **Topology** | Extrude as an interactive drag with numeric entry, multi-patch and group normal; Bevel; Delete; Weld Selected with threshold and Weld Target; Attach and Detach; Mirror to Whole (delete the instance, copy, mirror, attach, weld, report the centre-vertex count); Add Quad on an open edge; Subdivide with Propagate; Hide / Unhide patches respected by the overlay; Select Open Edges as a real selection. |
| **Surface properties** | Material ID per patch with Select by ID; the 32 smoothing-group buttons with Select by Group, Clear All and Auto Smooth by angle; Flip patch. |
| **Skeleton** | Add, delete, rename, re-parent bones and export the result; a naming guard on the standard names; Symmetrize; numeric bone size; the wing and arm stubs protected. |
| **Skin** | Pick a bone by clicking it or from the list; Assign, Remove, Select bone vertices; the overlay coloured by bone with orange for anything unassigned; Topology transfer from another creature by anchor patch and edge (vertex owners, material IDs, UVs). |
| **Limbs** | The Limb list with Create, Delete, Rename, the classification flags, patches and bones per limb, visibility cycling, and assigned patches fading as you go; validation against the 15-name table. |
| **Connections** | The Connection list with Create, Delete, flags, and a guided anchor-vertex mode: candidate vertices shown, click clockwise from the top left, auto-close on the start, live count against the 6 / 4 rule; Parent Bone by clicking. |
| **Animation** | Actions as now, plus a required-set checklist, loop and right-foot-forward hints, a half-cycle mirror with offset, and Import Rotations from another creature by bone name. |
| **Texturing** | A material navigator for the 18 IDs (names, bitmap per ID, size table, Null slots); UV editing in place on the creature instead of a detached cage; planar fit by axis per ID; 8-bit and power-of-two checks. |
| **Export** | Export SGM as now, plus the rulebook validator (orientation, size, seam and weld checks, torso vertex count, Auto Interior, material table, animation set). The .ebp / .lua / .lsc package stays a separate pipeline step (decided 2026-09-13); .ebp files open for their geometry. |

## 5. What Blender already gives us

- Undo, orthographic quad view, numeric input, the measure tool, reference images, hide and disable-select: Blender already has every scene-level convenience the guide spends pages on.
- Box and lasso select, proportional editing, snapping, X-mirror editing: all work on the pool today.
- Armature editing, Symmetrize with _l/_r names, keyframing, Auto Key, the NLA, Paste X-Flipped: the animation chapter is largely covered once the export side keeps up.
- The UV editor and image paint: the unwrap and paint chapters need integration, not new tools.
